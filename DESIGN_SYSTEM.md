# Design System

Curated by Carly draws from the understated refinement of Dior boutiques, The Row, Apple, Celine, Hermès, and modern luxury editorial magazines. Its visual language should feel composed, tactile, and quietly confident—never ornamental for ornament's sake.

## Design Philosophy

The application should feel calm, sophisticated, timeless, luxurious, and intentionally restrained.

Users should feel like they are browsing a private luxury wardrobe rather than using inventory software. Every screen should create space for the wardrobe itself to be seen, considered, and enjoyed. Functionality should be clear, but it should recede behind an editorial, highly considered experience.

## Color Palette

The palette is neutral, warm, and highly controlled. It should support imagery and clothing without competing for attention.

### Primary

- White — the principal canvas; clean, airy, and gallery-like.
- Black — used for typography, key actions, and confident contrast.
- Warm ivory — a softened neutral for surfaces and moments of warmth.
- Soft charcoal — a gentler alternative to pure black for secondary text and quiet structure.

### Accent

- Muted taupe — for subtle depth and tonal variation.
- Deep forest green — reserved for refined emphasis, status, or seasonal moments.
- Soft gold — used very sparingly for exceptional details or highlights.

Avoid bright colors. Accents should never overwhelm the neutral foundation or distract from the clothing and imagery.

## Typography

Typography should be elegant, deliberate, and easy to read. It should balance an editorial sensibility with the clarity expected of a modern digital product.

- Use large, confident headings with generous surrounding space.
- Keep body copy minimal, concise, and legible.
- Favor considered line lengths and comfortable line height.
- Let hierarchy come from scale, weight, spacing, and restraint rather than excessive decoration.
- Avoid crowded labels, unnecessary explanatory copy, and visually noisy type treatments.

## Motion

Motion should reinforce calmness and quality, never call attention to itself.

- Use subtle fade animations for entrances and content changes.
- Use gentle hover states that provide quiet feedback.
- Keep durations natural and restrained.
- Favor opacity, small shifts, and soft tonal changes over dramatic transforms.
- Avoid flashy transitions, bouncy motion, or attention-seeking effects.

## Photography

Imagery is central to the product experience and should be treated with the care of an editorial layout.

- Use large imagery wherever it improves storytelling or garment discovery.
- Favor editorial composition, natural texture, and thoughtful framing.
- Give images generous breathing room.
- Present clothing as considered pieces in a personal collection, not as inventory thumbnails.
- Avoid cluttered compositions, inconsistent crops, and imagery that feels overly commercial or generic.

## UI Principles

- Use large margins and intentional spacing to create a gallery-like rhythm.
- Use rounded corners with restraint and consistent proportions.
- Keep borders minimal, soft, and purposeful.
- Let surfaces feel tactile and calm rather than boxed-in or overly segmented.
- Design cards as museum display pieces rather than software panels.
- Prioritize one clear action at a time.
- Use icons only when they improve comprehension; never as decoration or a substitute for hierarchy.
- Every interaction should feel premium, responsive, and considered.

## What We Avoid

- Busy dashboards
- Dense tables
- Overuse of icons
- Bright gradients
- Corporate software aesthetics
- Excessive controls, labels, borders, and visual chrome
- Loud animations or novelty effects

Everything should feel closer to Dior than Salesforce.
