# Curated Brand Bible

**Status:** Strategic foundation, version 0.1  
**Purpose:** Define the company Curated should become before naming, logo, or visual identity development.  
**Source of truth:** This document synthesizes the existing product, experience, privacy, personalization, shopping, calendar, and AI strategy in this repository. It does not replace those specifications.

## The Decision

Curated should become a private style house for the individual.

It is not a closet inventory, productivity tool, shopping marketplace, trend feed, or AI novelty. It is a long-term companion that helps a person dress, collect, remember, travel, and evolve with greater discernment.

The technology should be extraordinarily capable and almost socially invisible. The relationship should feel less like operating software and more like being known by an exceptional hostess and stylist: observant, prepared, discreet, cultivated, and never overbearing.

The company is therefore not fundamentally in the business of wardrobe management. It is in the business of **personal continuity through style**.

Clothing is the first medium. Over time, the brand may earn permission to interpret adjacent parts of a well-lived life, but it should not expand beyond the wardrobe until it has become genuinely trusted there.

## Founding Belief

A wardrobe is not an inventory. It is a living record of taste, memory, aspiration, place, and change.

Most fashion technology treats clothing as one of three things:

- merchandise to sell;
- content to consume;
- data to organize.

Curated should treat clothing as part of a life.

The company exists to help people see what they already possess, use it beautifully, add to it selectively, and recognize how their taste evolves. Its commercial success must never depend on making the customer feel deficient, unfashionable, or behind.

## The Enduring Promise

**Curated helps you live beautifully with what you own—and choose with care what comes next.**

This promise accommodates the full product:

- the wardrobe reveals and remembers what is owned;
- Dress My Day applies it to real life;
- the Style Archive preserves identity and memory;
- History creates continuity;
- Packing carries personal style into travel;
- Personal Shopper protects discernment at the moment of purchase;
- the profile and stylist engine learn with humility as the customer changes.

## Category

### Category to build

**The private style house**

This is a more ambitious and emotionally resonant category than “AI wardrobe app” or “digital closet.” A style house combines the authority and taste of a fashion house with the intimacy and allegiance of a private household.

### Functional description

When clarity is required, use:

> A private wardrobe and personal styling companion.

“AI” may explain how the product works, but should not lead the consumer brand. AI is infrastructure, not identity. “Luxury” should be demonstrated through conduct and experience rather than repeatedly claimed.

### Competitive frame

Curated should not compete on how many garments it can catalog, how many products it can recommend, or how quickly it can generate an outfit. It should compete on:

- quality of judgment;
- depth of personal understanding;
- discretion and trust;
- emotional and aesthetic richness;
- continuity across years;
- the courage to recommend restraint.

## The Customer Relationship

Curated should occupy a rare role: **the trusted keeper of personal style**.

The customer is not a “user,” fashion novice, conversion target, or dataset. She is the principal of the house. Curated is in service to her life and taste.

The relationship should progress through four stages:

1. **Welcome** — Curated is immediately useful without demanding an exhaustive profile.
2. **Recognition** — It begins to notice patterns and remember choices accurately.
3. **Trust** — It proves discretion, practical judgment, and the ability to say “no.”
4. **Continuity** — It becomes an archive and companion through seasons, travels, roles, and changes in taste.

Emotional attachment will come from accumulated recognition, not artificial intimacy. The product should never pretend to know the customer better than the evidence allows.

## The Brand Idea

### Central idea

**A life, beautifully considered.**

This is an internal organizing idea, not automatically the public tagline. It joins utility with romance: Curated considers the rain, the walk, the dress code, the packed suitcase, the loved coat, the changed silhouette, and the feeling the customer wants that day.

### Emotional territory

Curated should evoke:

- entering a beloved townhouse where every object has a story;
- being expected, remembered, and cared for;
- choosing a dress in a private room before dinner;
- opening a cedar wardrobe or a linen-lined drawer;
- garden roses after rain;
- handwritten notes tucked into books;
- inherited confidence rather than performed status;
- the pleasure of returning, not the anxiety of keeping up.

### The governing aesthetic tension

**Maximalist with restraint.**

This does not mean placing ornament everywhere. It means richness in the world and discipline in the composition.

- Maximalism belongs in material, photography, narrative, collecting, color, and detail.
- Restraint belongs in hierarchy, interaction, pacing, editing, and behavior.

The product may feel layered without becoming crowded. It may be feminine without becoming juvenile, romantic without becoming nostalgic pastiche, and luxurious without becoming sterile or status-obsessed.

## Strategic Challenges to the Current Assumptions

### 1. “Quiet luxury” is too narrow and too culturally temporary

The existing documents use “quiet luxury,” white space, and Dior-boutique minimalism as shorthand for quality. Those principles were useful safeguards against generic software, but they should not remain the brand’s central concept.

Curated’s desired world is warmer, more collected, more literary, and more emotionally specific. The new doctrine is not visual excess. It is **cultivated abundance, rigorously edited**.

### 2. A Dior boutique is a useful quality bar, not an ownable world

Building the brand around another house’s codes will make Curated feel derivative. References such as Cabana, House & Garden UK, AERIN, Sotheby’s, and The World of Interiors should be analyzed for principles—not combined into a mood-board collage.

Curated must ultimately possess its own rooms, rituals, language, symbols, and point of view.

### 3. “By Carly” creates warmth but may constrain scale

“Curated by Carly” currently serves three different purposes: company name, founder signature, and personalized product grammar (“By {first name}”). Those should be separated.

The founder can remain an important source of taste and trust without making every customer feel she is borrowing Carly’s wardrobe. The personalized construction is promising, but it needs testing: “Curated / By Anna” may communicate authorship and agency, while it may also confuse who is doing the curating.

No naming decision should be finalized until linguistic, trademark, domain, search, international, and customer-perception work is complete. “Curated” is elegant but descriptive and potentially difficult to own.

### 4. Englishness must be atmosphere, not costume or exclusion

The English rose garden and townhouse are powerful founding metaphors. Used literally, they could become stage-set branding, encode a narrow class fantasy, or suggest that taste belongs to one nationality and social tradition.

The transferable values are hospitality, cultivation, eccentricity, stewardship, wit, patina, and ease. The house should be English in spirit but worldly in collection, contemporary in ethics, and open in invitation.

### 5. Luxury is a standard of care, not a demographic filter

The product can be aspirational without assuming a designer wardrobe, extreme wealth, a particular body, or a high-consumption lifestyle. Rewearing, small wardrobes, budget limits, mobility needs, and imperfect metadata are not edge cases. Treating them beautifully is part of the luxury proposition.

### 6. The brand must resist becoming an affiliate engine

Personal Shopper is strategically distinctive because it can say “Pass” or “Wait.” If monetization rewards purchase volume, the brand promise will collapse. Any future commerce model must preserve neutral judgment and make conflicts legible.

## Brand Principles

### 1. Discernment over abundance

More options are not automatically more valuable. Curated edits, compares, and chooses.

### 2. Recognition without surveillance

The experience becomes personal through permission, evidence, and correction. Privacy is part of hospitality.

### 3. Romance grounded in reality

Beauty never overrides rain, walking, fit, availability, comfort, or the actual occasion.

### 4. Stewardship over consumption

The first question is what the customer already owns. Shopping is an occasional extension of a wardrobe, not the product’s center of gravity.

### 5. Evolution without erasure

Taste changes. Curated notices change without declaring a permanent identity or deleting the past.

### 6. Confidence without authority theater

Curated is decisive when the evidence is strong and candid when it is not. It does not use AI certainty to disguise missing knowledge.

### 7. Hospitality in every detail

Permissions, errors, empty states, loading, deletion, and account recovery deserve the same care as the editorial surfaces.

## Personality

Curated is:

- cultivated, never performatively cultured;
- warm, never effusive;
- observant, never invasive;
- assured, never absolute;
- feminine, never diminutive;
- romantic, never sentimental;
- worldly, never status-struck;
- witty, never flippant;
- exacting, never cold;
- contemporary, never trend-chasing.

### Archetypal role

The primary archetype is **The Hostess**: she welcomes, remembers, anticipates, and creates the conditions for confidence.

The supporting archetypes are:

- **The Curator** — exercises judgment and creates meaning through selection;
- **The Keeper** — protects memory, privacy, and continuity;
- **The Companion** — travels alongside a changing life without taking it over.

## Voice

### Voice standard

Write like a perceptive, well-read woman with excellent judgment and nothing to prove. She is concise because she has considered the matter, not because the interface demands fragments.

### Voice behaviors

- Make a clear recommendation.
- Give specific reasons rooted in the customer’s life and wardrobe.
- Prefer sensory, precise language to luxury clichés.
- Use warmth through attention, not exclamation marks.
- Acknowledge uncertainty gracefully.
- Invite correction without surrendering all judgment.
- Treat rewearing and restraint positively.

### Language to favor

Consider, keep, return to, compose, collect, wear, remember, prepare, accompany, edit, archive, house, wardrobe, room, note, chapter, season, occasion.

### Language to avoid

Optimize, hack, must-have, haul, unlock, elevate, game-changing, obsessed, effortless when effort is actually required, perfect for everyone, problem areas, age-appropriate, flattering as a euphemism for smaller, and unsupported claims such as “always” or “definitely.”

### Example

Instead of:

> We generated three optimized looks for your calendar.

Use:

> For the meeting and dinner, I would keep the ivory blouse and navy trousers as the foundation. The loafers are the wiser choice for the walk; add the velvet jacket before dinner if you want the evening to feel more distinct.

## Experience Doctrine

### The house, not the dashboard

The experience should be structured as a sequence of rooms and rituals, not a grid of features. This is a conceptual model; labels should remain clear and usable.

- **The Hall** — arrival and orientation;
- **The Wardrobe** — owned pieces and their stories;
- **Today’s Edit** — the daily consultation;
- **The Dressing Room** — consideration, comparison, and future shopping decisions;
- **The Style Archive** — remembered looks and chapters;
- **The Travelling Wardrobe** — packing and place;
- **The Study** — profile, preferences, privacy, and what Curated has learned.

This metaphor should guide pacing, transitions, content, and art direction. It should not produce obscure navigation or theatrical UI copy at the expense of comprehension.

### Signature rituals

The brand should create repeatable moments customers anticipate:

1. **The Morning Edit** — one considered answer for the actual day.
2. **The Wardrobe Welcome** — newly added pieces are received, described, and placed with care.
3. **The Second Look** — a pause before purchase: Buy, Consider, Wait, or Pass.
4. **The Packing Table** — a trip composed as a small wardrobe story, not a checklist alone.
5. **The Seasonal Turning** — a reflective edit of what was worn, loved, neglected, repaired, or released.
6. **The Style Chapter** — a humane recognition that the customer’s taste is changing.

### Luxury service standards

- Never make the customer repeat known information without reason.
- Ask at most one consequential question at a time.
- Preserve manual paths when integrations fail.
- Explain why access is requested and what will not be collected.
- Make correction, export, disconnection, and deletion dignified and easy.
- Never create false urgency.
- Never turn a small wardrobe into a sales opportunity.
- Never allow the brand world to obscure accessibility or task clarity.

## Visual World: Direction Before Design

The visual identity should not be designed until the strategic and naming gates are complete. When it begins, it should explore the following principles.

### Composition

- editorial asymmetry anchored by rigorous grids;
- generous quiet around moments of concentrated richness;
- layering through scale, crop, texture, and depth rather than interface clutter;
- pages that feel paced and inhabited, not templated.

### Color

Move beyond a predominantly white-and-black “luxury app” palette. The core world should investigate:

- garden and bottle greens;
- old rose, faded damask, oxblood, aubergine, and tobacco;
- parchment, linen, plaster, and candlelight neutrals;
- lacquered darks and occasional mineral or floral notes;
- metal tones used as material, not gradient decoration.

The palette must remain a system, not a collection of romantic colors. Clothing photography must retain visual authority.

### Material and texture

Potential cues include linen, silk moiré, aged paper, botanical engraving, lacquer, chintz, velvet, wood grain, silver, and hand-painted detail. Digital translation should be subtle and performant; literal skeuomorphism and faux-vintage filters are off-brand.

### Typography

The eventual typography should combine editorial character with intimate readability. Avoid the predictable formula of a high-contrast fashion serif paired with a generic geometric sans unless the execution becomes distinctly ownable. Typography must work beautifully in recommendations, garment metadata, long reading, numbers, and privacy controls—not only in campaign headlines.

### Photography

Photography should build a lived world around clothing:

- rooms, hands, dressing rituals, gardens, travel traces, books, objects, and garments in use;
- directional natural light, atmosphere, patina, and human presence;
- a mixture of still life, portrait, documentary detail, and editorial fashion;
- casting across age, body, ethnicity, gender expression, wardrobe size, and ways of living;
- beauty that feels collected and inhabited rather than rented for a shoot.

### Symbols and graphic language

Future exploration may draw from cataloguing, bookplates, house marks, garden plans, correspondence, provenance labels, and archival notation. These are territories to investigate, not instructions to place crests, monograms, roses, or keys into a logo.

## Brand Architecture

The masterbrand should remain primary. Product experiences should feel like rooms or services within one house, not independent sub-brands.

Recommended working hierarchy:

- **Curated** — masterbrand working title;
  - Today’s Edit;
  - Wardrobe;
  - Style Archive;
  - Packing / Travelling Wardrobe;
  - Personal Shopper / Dressing Room;
  - Profile and learned style record.

Avoid naming every feature with ornamental language. Use evocative titles for signature rituals and editorial storytelling; use plain labels where customers need immediate certainty.

## Trust Is a Brand System

The repository’s privacy architecture is not merely compliance. It is one of the brand’s most credible luxury behaviors.

Curated should publicly commit to:

- private-by-default wardrobes, photos, measurements, schedules, and conversations;
- clear permission before calendar, location, or external context is used;
- minimal collection and bounded retention;
- no sale of personal style or wardrobe data;
- no advertising based on private wardrobe data;
- explainable recommendations and reviewable learned preferences;
- easy export, reset, disconnection, and deletion;
- no body judgment or inferred measurements from photographs;
- no retailer influence hidden inside styling advice.

These commitments should eventually become a short, beautifully written public covenant, supported by exact legal and technical truth.

## Commercial Doctrine

The business model must reward long-term trust and product quality.

### Aligned models

- membership or subscription for ongoing concierge value;
- premium service tiers with transparent differences;
- paid human consultations or special projects when operationally credible;
- carefully disclosed commerce that does not alter the recommendation;
- selective brand collaborations governed by editorial fit and customer benefit;
- archival, travel, or household services that deepen the core relationship.

### Dangerous models

- advertising against private wardrobe or calendar context;
- pay-to-rank product recommendations;
- affiliate economics as the primary revenue engine;
- engagement mechanics designed to increase browsing or purchasing;
- selling aggregate trend intelligence derived from private customer behavior without meaningful consent;
- constant promotions that train the customer to distrust price and judgment.

The brand should be willing to grow more slowly in order to preserve the independence of its advice.

## Marketing Doctrine

### What marketing should sell

Not “AI outfits in seconds.” Sell the feeling and consequence:

- being ready without feeling rushed;
- remembering the life attached to clothing;
- traveling as oneself;
- buying less and liking it more;
- feeling recognized without being watched;
- returning to one’s wardrobe with renewed imagination.

### Content territories

1. **The Life of a Wardrobe** — memory, repair, repetition, provenance, and evolution.
2. **The Art of the Edit** — how thoughtful choices are made.
3. **Rooms and Rituals** — dressing spaces, travel, gardens, books, fragrance, and objects as context for style.
4. **Women of Discernment** — intimate profiles centered on judgment and life, not consumption.
5. **Letters from the House** — seasonal editorial correspondence with genuine usefulness.
6. **The Second Look** — culturally intelligent arguments for waiting, passing, repairing, or choosing well.

### Launch posture

Launch should feel like an invitation into a world, not a software announcement. Early access should be small enough to deliver hospitality and learn the rituals that create loyalty. The strongest early proof will be customers saying, “It knows why this works for my life,” not a large waitlist or volume of garments uploaded.

### Community

Do not begin with an open social feed. The private nature of Curated is differentiating. If community emerges, it should begin through editorial participation, salons, letters, trusted referrals, and opt-in sharing of selected stories—never default exposure of wardrobes or bodies.

## Growth Guardrails

Curated may expand only when the new offering:

1. deepens the customer’s ability to live with personal style;
2. benefits from the trust and context already earned;
3. can meet the same standards of privacy and judgment;
4. does not turn the house into a marketplace or lifestyle miscellany;
5. has a credible reason to come from Curated rather than any luxury brand.

Adjacent categories such as fragrance, interiors, objects, travel, or beauty may fit the emotional world but are not automatically strategic extensions. The company must first earn authority through the wardrobe.

## Measures of Brand Health

Do not rely on engagement or conversion alone. Track:

- voluntary return to Today’s Edit;
- recommendation wear and thoughtful modification;
- customer-reported confidence, usefulness, and feeling understood;
- successful “Wait” and “Pass” outcomes in Personal Shopper;
- continued use of existing pieces and healthy rewearing;
- archive creation and return over long periods;
- permission opt-in after clear explanation, not through coercion;
- correction and deletion flows completed without friction;
- referrals described in relational language (“I trust it,” “it knows me”);
- retention across life changes and seasons;
- qualitative evidence that customers perceive a distinctive world without needing the reference list explained.

## The Branding Sprint

This work should proceed in gates. A later phase begins only when the prior decision is strong enough to constrain it.

### Phase 0 — Founding alignment

**Purpose:** Decide the company we are willing to build and the compromises we will refuse.

Work:

- founder interviews on ambition, taste, biography, business model, and non-negotiables;
- reconcile “Curated,” “Curated by Carly,” and “By {first name}” roles;
- define intended audience without using wealth or age as a substitute for psychographics;
- agree on commercial and privacy covenants;
- choose the brand’s relationship to the founder over the next ten years;
- document expansion boundaries.

Outputs:

- approved founding belief, promise, category, customer relationship, and guardrails;
- founder-role decision;
- strategic questions for research;
- version 0.2 of this document.

Gate: leadership can describe what Curated is, what it is not, and why it deserves to exist without naming features or references.

### Phase 1 — Cultural and customer intelligence

**Purpose:** Replace projection with evidence while protecting the ambition.

Work:

- interview customers and aspirational non-customers about dressing, memory, privacy, shopping regret, travel, and trusted advice;
- observe wardrobe and purchase-decision rituals with consent;
- map direct, adjacent, and cultural competitors;
- audit the chosen references for transferable principles and overused codes;
- study luxury hospitality, private banking, archival services, publishing, and membership brands for trust behaviors;
- identify cultural, class, gender, and accessibility risks in the townhouse metaphor.

Outputs:

- audience tensions and jobs-to-be-felt;
- category and whitespace map;
- cultural risk and inclusion brief;
- evidence-based brand opportunity.

Gate: the brand idea is both emotionally desired and distinct from existing fashion technology.

### Phase 2 — Positioning, naming, and architecture

**Purpose:** Create legally and linguistically ownable foundations.

Work:

- finalize positioning and message hierarchy;
- develop naming territories, including but not limited to the working title;
- conduct preliminary trademark, company-name, domain, search, pronunciation, and international screening with qualified counsel where needed;
- test founder endorsement and personalized naming structures;
- define masterbrand, feature, editorial, and future-service architecture;
- develop the public trust covenant.

Outputs:

- approved name or disciplined decision to retain the working title;
- positioning statement and message house;
- brand architecture;
- naming system;
- trust covenant draft.

Gate: the name can be owned, spoken, remembered, and extended without explaining the mood board.

### Phase 3 — Verbal identity and story

**Purpose:** Make the company recognizable without visuals.

Work:

- codify voice dimensions and situational range;
- write origin story, manifesto, one-line description, short and long narratives;
- define vocabulary, syntax, naming conventions, and prohibited clichés;
- write representative product moments: welcome, recommendation, uncertainty, error, permission, purchase restraint, packing, archive, and deletion;
- develop editorial franchises and launch narrative.

Outputs:

- verbal identity system;
- messaging library;
- product voice patterns;
- editorial charter;
- launch narrative.

Gate: unattributed copy feels unmistakably Curated across romance, utility, and trust-sensitive moments.

### Phase 4 — Creative territories

**Purpose:** Explore distinct worlds before choosing an identity system.

Develop at least three coherent territories, each expressing the strategy through:

- art direction;
- typography;
- color and material;
- photography and film;
- graphic language;
- motion and sound;
- digital application;
- correspondence, packaging, and physical hospitality.

Each territory must be tested in real product states, not only campaign mockups: a rainy workday recommendation, a nearly empty wardrobe, a calendar permission, a “Pass” decision, a private archive, and an error state.

Outputs:

- three strategic creative territories;
- comparative evaluation against distinctiveness, longevity, accessibility, emotional fit, and product usability;
- selected territory with rationale.

Gate: the chosen world is ownable without literal roses, borrowed fashion-house codes, or generic luxury minimalism.

### Phase 5 — Identity system

**Purpose:** Build a complete behavior and expression system. Logo development begins here, not before.

Work:

- wordmark, symbol if justified, monogram or house mark if useful;
- typography, color, composition, image, illustration, icon, motion, and sound systems;
- digital material and texture rules;
- accessibility and performance standards;
- identity behavior across product, editorial, social, film, correspondence, events, partnerships, and future physical touchpoints;
- legal review and production testing.

Outputs:

- complete identity system;
- asset library and templates;
- design tokens and product migration guidance;
- photography and production standards.

Gate: the identity works as beautifully in a consent dialog and wardrobe card as it does in a campaign.

### Phase 6 — Experience translation

**Purpose:** Make the brand true in the product rather than applied to it.

Work:

- redesign the journey around rooms, rituals, and service standards;
- prioritize Morning Edit, Wardrobe Welcome, Second Look, Packing Table, and Style Chapter prototypes;
- align AI behavior, confidence, and explanations with the verbal identity;
- define onboarding, learning, permissions, privacy, deletion, and failure as hospitality moments;
- run accessibility, usability, privacy-comprehension, and emotional-response testing;
- build a phased migration plan that protects functional stability.

Outputs:

- signature-experience prototypes;
- product-content system;
- service blueprint;
- implementation sequence and acceptance criteria.

Gate: customers experience the promise through behavior even when decorative brand elements are removed.

### Phase 7 — Introduction and stewardship

**Purpose:** Enter culture deliberately and preserve coherence over time.

Work:

- invite a small founding membership cohort;
- create high-touch onboarding and feedback rituals;
- launch editorial and referral programs;
- establish partnership and commerce review standards;
- form a recurring brand council spanning founder, brand, product, AI, privacy, accessibility, and customer care;
- review brand health through qualitative and longitudinal evidence.

Outputs:

- introduction plan;
- founding-member program;
- partnership rubric;
- brand governance calendar;
- version 1.0 of the Brand Bible.

Gate: growth can occur without weakening advice, privacy, hospitality, or aesthetic coherence.

## Decisions Still Required

This strategic foundation intentionally does not settle:

- the final company and product name;
- whether Carly is the permanent public founder-endorsed signature;
- the primary customer segment and geographic launch market;
- price and membership structure;
- whether commerce or affiliate revenue will ever be accepted;
- the final tagline;
- the visual identity, logo, monogram, palette, or typefaces;
- the role of human stylists alongside the AI service;
- the boundary between private utility and public editorial media;
- how far the house may eventually extend beyond wardrobe and style.

These are not gaps to fill casually. They are the work of the first three phases.

## The Standard

Curated will become iconic only if its point of view survives contact with product decisions, commercial pressure, and time.

The standard is not whether every screen looks luxurious. The standard is whether every choice makes the customer feel that her life has been considered with taste, intelligence, discretion, and care.

If Curated can make technology feel like hospitality, privacy feel like luxury, restraint feel desirable, and a wardrobe feel like a living personal archive, it can become more than a useful application.

It can become a house people remain with.
